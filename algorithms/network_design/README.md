# Network Design (5 algorithms)

Decide where warehouses go and how demand maps to them.

**Levels:** 2 beginner · 0 intermediate · 3 advanced. Every page follows the same rhythm: plain English, a runnable example, a "check yourself" recall test, and a small challenge. No math beyond +, -, ×, ÷.

Read top to bottom the first time - the order goes from easiest to hardest.

| # | Algorithm | Level | One-line idea |
|---|-----------|-------|---------------|
| 1 | [Break-Even Analysis](break_even_analysis.md) | Beginner | How many units must we sell before the business stops losing money? Fixed costs divided by the margin per unit |
| 2 | [Center of Gravity Location](center_of_gravity.md) | Beginner | Where should the warehouse go? The center of gravity weights every customer's location by demand volume and returns the balance point - the spot that minimizes total straight-line shipping effort |
| 3 | [Facility Location (Fixed Cost)](facility_location_fixed_cost.md) | Advanced | The real siting problem: each candidate warehouse has a fixed opening cost, shipping costs vary by lane, and you can't open everything |
| 4 | [Network Reliability](network_reliability.md) | Advanced | Each node works with some probability - what's the chance the NETWORK still functions? This combines component reliabilities and backup edges into one survival probability |
| 5 | [Single-Source Allocation](single_source_allocation.md) | Advanced | Each customer must be served by exactly ONE facility - no splitting shipments |
