3. Where does 'takt' come from?

**OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness)** (`oee`)

4. A = 90%, P = 90%, Q = 90%. Is OEE 90%?
5. Which OEE factor do minor stops and slow cycling hit?
6. Why is OEE better than just 'uptime'?

**Cycle Time Efficiency (PCE)** (`cycle_time_efficiency`)

7. What are the two ingredients of PCE?
8. Typical PCE in unimproved processes is...?
9. You halve waiting time. What happens to PCE?

**Little's Law: WIP** (`wip_calculation`)

10. Throughput 20/hour, flow time 30 minutes. WIP?

Total: 12 questions.

## Part 2 - Answer key

1. It halves - the line must beat twice as fast, or you add capacity/time.
2. No - it builds inventory nobody ordered (the worst lean waste).
3. The German word for baton/metronome - the conductor's beat for the whole orchestra.
4. No - multiply them: 0.9 x 0.9 x 0.9 = about 73%. Three 'good' numbers hide a big overall loss.
5. Performance - the machine runs but slower than ideal.
6. Uptime ignores speed and quality - a machine can be 'up', running slowly, making scrap, and uptime still says 100%.
7. Value-added time (actual transformation) and total cycle time (everything, including all waiting).
8. Often under 5% - most of the time, work is waiting, not being done.
9. Roughly doubles - waiting sits in the denominator, so attacking it lifts efficiency directly.
10. 10 - convert units first: 0.5 hour x 20/hour = 10 units.
11. Flow time - shorten queues and waits.
12. Cash sits on the floor, defects hide longer, and lead times stretch - WIP is the smell of blocked flow.
