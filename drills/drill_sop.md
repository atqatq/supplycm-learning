# Recall Drill: S&OP

Answer from memory first, then check the key at the bottom.
Score 1 point per correct answer. Anything you miss goes back on your flashcard rotation.

## Part 1 - Questions

**Demand-Supply Match** (`demand_supply_match`)

1. What are the two gap directions and their risks?
2. Demand exceeds supply in month 3. Name two honest fixes.
3. Who belongs in the room when gaps appear?

**Chase Strategy** (`production_chase_strategy`)

4. What does chase do to inventory and to workforce stress?
5. When is chase the obvious choice?
6. What does a chase plan look like in peak season hiring?

**Level Strategy** (`production_level_strategy`)

7. What does level strategy stabilize, and what swings instead?
8. Demand is seasonal and shelf life is 5 days. Level or chase?
9. How do you find the level rate?

Total: 9 questions.

## Part 2 - Answer key

1. Short (missed sales, firefighting) and surplus (cash on shelves, markdowns).
2. Pull production forward / add capacity, or shape demand - raise price, defer promotions, pre-book orders.
3. Sales, operations, and finance together - S&OP is the meeting where those gaps get settled.
4. Inventory stays minimal; workforce/capacity stress peaks because output must flex every period.
5. Fresh food, made-to-order, or when holding stock is costlier than flexing capacity.
6. Hire temps ahead of the ramp, then release them - expensive and socially costly if extreme.
7. Output and staffing stay constant; inventory levels swing with demand.
8. Chase - building ahead is impossible with short shelf life.
9. A common start: average demand over the horizon, then adjust for ending-inventory targets and capacity limits.
