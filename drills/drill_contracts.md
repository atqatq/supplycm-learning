# Recall Drill: Contracts

Answer from memory first, then check the key at the bottom.

## Part 1 - Questions

**Revenue Sharing Contract** (`revenue_sharing_contract`)

1. What does the buyer give up in exchange for a lower wholesale price?
2. Why does revenue sharing reduce the buyer's overstock pain?
3. Name the classic industry for this contract.

**Buyback Contract** (`buyback_contract`)

4. Who takes on the leftover risk under a buyback?
5. Why do total supply chain profits often rise?
6. What happens if the buyback price is set too high?

**Quantity Flexibility Contract** (`quantity_flexibility_contract`)

7. What can the buyer change under this contract?
8. Why does the supplier accept flexibility?
9. Flexibility 5% vs 30% - trade-off?

Total: 9 questions.

## Part 2 - Answer key

1. A slice of every sale's revenue - the supplier trades margin now for a stake in outcomes.
2. Less capital is tied per unit, so unsold stock hurts less - the buyer can afford more inventory.
3. Video rentals in the 1990s - studios took shares of rental revenue so stores could stock many copies.
4. The supplier - it pays for returns, which is why it can afford the higher wholesale price it charges.
5. The buyer orders closer to the true demand, so fewer sales are lost to empty shelves.
6. The buyer over-orders carelessly - returns pile up and the supplier's margin evaporates.
7. The order size, within a band (e.g., 20% below to 20% above the original commitment).
8. It charges for the option (usually a higher wholesale price) and gains a committed base order.
9. 5% barely helps the buyer; 30% protects them but the supplier prices that risk in - the sweet spot is negotiated, not maximal.
