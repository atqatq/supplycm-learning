# Glossary

Simple definitions of supply chain terms used in these lessons.

## A

**ABC Analysis**: Sorting items by value. A items = top 80% of value, B = 15%, C = 5%.

**AHP (Analytic Hierarchy Process)**: A method for making decisions with multiple criteria by comparing them in pairs.

**Assignment Problem**: Matching workers to jobs at minimum cost.

**Availability**: Percentage of time equipment is running (part of OEE).

## B

**Backorder**: An order that cannot be filled immediately but will be filled later.

**Buyback Contract**: Supplier agrees to buy back unsold inventory at a set price.

**Bullwhip Effect**: Small demand changes at the customer cause bigger changes upstream.

## C

**Carbon Footprint**: Amount of CO2 produced by an activity.

**Chase Strategy**: Production matches demand each period.

**Cp/Cpk**: Measures of process capability.

**Cross-Docking**: Moving goods from receiving to shipping without storage.

**Croston's Method**: Forecasting method for intermittent (sparse) demand.

**Cycle Time**: Total time to complete one unit.

**Cycle Time Efficiency**: Value-added time divided by total cycle time.

## D

**Demand**: How much customers want to buy.

**DPMO**: Defects Per Million Opportunities.

**DMAIC**: Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control (Six Sigma process).

**Double Marginalization**: When both supplier and retailer add margins, price is too high.

## E

**EOQ (Economic Order Quantity)**: The order size that minimizes total inventory cost.

**Exponential Smoothing**: Forecast method that weights recent data more.

## F

**Fill Rate**: Percentage of demand filled from stock.

**Forecast**: A prediction of future demand.

## H

**Holding Cost**: Cost of keeping inventory (storage, insurance, etc.).

**Holt-Winters**: Forecasting method for data with trend and seasonality.

**Hungarian Algorithm**: Solves the assignment problem optimally.

## I

**Inventory**: Stuff kept in storage.

**Intermittent Demand**: Demand that is sporadic with many zero periods.

## K

**Kraljic Matrix**: Tool for sorting suppliers by risk and profit impact.

**Kanban**: Card-based pull system where each card authorizes one container of parts.

## L

**Lead Time**: Time from placing an order to receiving it.

**Lean**: Doing more with less by removing waste.

**Level Strategy**: Constant production rate.

**Little's Law**: WIP = Throughput x Flow Time.

## M

**MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error)**: Forecast accuracy measure.

**Moving Average**: Average of the last N data points.

**MRP (Material Requirements Planning)**: Computing what materials are needed and when.

## O

**OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness)**: Availability x Performance x Quality.

**Ordering Cost**: Cost of placing an order.

## P

**P-chart**: Control chart for proportion defective.

**Pegging**: Tracing a component requirement back to the parent order that caused it.

**Phantom BOM**: A logical grouping in a bill of materials that is never built or stocked as a real item.

**Provenance**: The history of a product through the supply chain.

## Q

**Quality**: Meeting customer expectations.

**Quantity Flexibility Contract**: Retailer can adjust order within a range.

## R

**Reorder Point**: Inventory level that triggers a new order.

**Revenue Sharing Contract**: Supplier gets a share of retailer's revenue.

**Reverse Logistics**: Moving products from customer back to supplier.

**Risk Pooling**: Aggregating inventory to reduce total safety stock.

**RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error)**: Forecast accuracy measure.

## S

**Safety Stock**: Extra inventory to protect against uncertainty.

**Seasonality**: A repeating pattern in demand.

**S&OP (Sales and Operations Planning)**: Monthly process to balance supply and demand.

**Service Level**: Probability of not stocking out.

**Sigma Level**: A quality measure (Six Sigma = 3.4 DPMO).

**Simple Moving Average**: Average of the last N data points.

**Simulation**: Testing decisions with a model.

**Six Sigma**: Quality goal of 3.4 defects per million.

**Supplier**: A company that provides materials or products.

**Supply Chain**: The journey of a product from raw materials to customer.

## T

**Takt Time**: The pace of customer demand (available time / demand).

**Throughput**: Rate at which products are produced.

**TOPSIS**: Method for ranking options by closeness to ideal.

**TSP (Traveling Salesman Problem)**: Finding the shortest route visiting all locations.

## V

**Value-Added Time**: Time spent actually improving the product.

**VRP (Vehicle Routing Problem)**: TSP with multiple vehicles and capacity.

## W

**WIP (Work In Progress)**: Inventory that is being processed.

**Warehouse**: A building for storing products.

## X

**X-bar Chart**: Control chart for monitoring process mean.

## Y

**Yield**: The share of production output that is usable (the rest is scrap or rework).

## Z

**Z-Score**: How many standard deviations a value sits from the average - the "surprise scale".

**ZOPA (Zone of Possible Agreement)**: The overlap between a buyer’s maximum and a seller’s minimum in a negotiation.
